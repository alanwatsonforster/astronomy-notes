# Celestron 8–24 mm Zoom Eyepiece

## Optical Design

Celestron don’t state the optical configuration, but Hawks says that the eyepiece has four elements in three groups. If that’s the case, then we expect to find two singlet lenses and one cemented doublet.

Following convention, we’ll order the lenses, groups, and surfaces in the order that light passes through them. So, the first group is closest to the telescope objective, the third group is closest to the eye, and the second group is in between these. Similarly, the first surface of a lens is closest to the telescope objective and the second surface is closest to the eye.

The barrel has an outer diameter of 1.25-inch (31.75 mm) and is about 24 mm long. The body has an outer diameter of about 46 mm and is 70 mm long (excluding the eye cup).

The third group is fixed. It is about 70 mm after the end of the barrel. It can be removed by unscrewing its holder from the eyepiece and appears to be a thin singlet. The clear aperture is about 22 mm. The first surface is convex, and the second surface is close to flat. Projecting a distant object on a screen, it appears to be a converging lens with a focal length of about 50 mm.

The first and second groups move on helicoils. The movement of the first group can be seen without disassembling the lens. The movement of the second is most clearly seen after removing the third group. As the eyepiece is zoomed from 24 mm to 8 mm, the first group moves away from the eye and the second group moves towards the eye. Mechanically, the range of the first group is about 7.5 mm and the range of the second group is about 33 mm. When zoomed to 24 mm, the distance between the first surface of the first group and the last surface of the second group is about 40 mm. When zoomed to 8 mm, the distance from the first surface of the first group to the last surface of the second group is about 80 mm. Thus, the separation of the two groups changes by about a factor of two as the eyepiece is zoomed from 24 mm to 8 mm.

Comparing the zoom eyepiece to other eyepieces with fixed aperture stops and similar focus positions, the first group appears to be before the focal plane of the telescope. The clear aperture of the first group is about 18 mm in diameter. The first surface is concave. Viewed from the front, the apparent clear aperture of the second group changes from about 9 mm in diameter when zoomed to 8 mm to about 16 mm in diameter when zoomed to 24 mm. This behavior suggests that the first group is diverging and is consistent with the change in separation between the first and second groups of about a factor of two measured mechanically.

The clear aperture of the second group is about 31 mm. The second surface of the second group is convex. Viewing through it, the apparent clear aperture of the first group changes from about 13 mm when the eyepiece is zoomed to 8 mm to more than 31 mm (limited by the clear aperture of the second group) when zoomed to 24 mm. The apparent clear aperture of the first group is equal to the clear aperture of the second group when the lens is zoomed to about 18 mm. This behavior suggests that the second group is converging.

Viewing from the eye side with the third group removed, the front of the first group is focused by the second group.

Viewed from the telescope side, the effective field stop is provided by the second group and has an apparent diameter of about 9 mm when zoomed to 8 mm and about 16 mm when zoomed to 24 mm. Viewed from the eye side, the field stop appears to be the ring against the first surface of the second group.

That the apparent size of the field stop changes by about a factor of two when the separation of the first and second groups increases by about 40 mm suggests that at 8 mm the separation of the first and second groups is about 40 mm and at 8 mm it is about 80 mm.

That the 31 mm clear aperture of the second group appears to be about 16 mm at 24 mm suggests that the focal length of the first group is about equal to the distance between the groups. Unless the lenses are very thick, the focal length is probably about 40 mm.

The field stop (and dust on a surface, perhaps the first surface of the second group) is in focus at 24 mm.

In summary, we have a diverging, moving first group, a converging, moving second group, and a converging, fixed third group.

First Set of Postulates:

- The first group acts as a variable Barlow lens. As it moves forwards, further from the eye, it increases the magnification and moves the focal plane backwards,
- The second group acts to maintain focus and to increase the magnification. As the focal plane of the first group moves backwards, this group also moves backwards. As it moves backwards, more of its power becomes important.
- The third group seals the lens and acts to correct chromatic aberration in the second group.

For a focal length of *f* \= 24 mm and a field stop of *s* \= 16 mm, the true field of view *θ* ≈ 2 arctan(*s*/2*f*) is *θ* ≈ 37 deg. This is in good agreement with the manufacturer’s specification of 40 deg. The slightly larger clear aperture of the first group acts to reduce vignetting.

For a focal length of *f* \= 8 mm and a field stop of *s* \= 9 mm, the true field of view *θ* is
*θ* ≈ 59 deg.
This is in good agreement with the manufacturer’s specification of 60 deg.

Comparing to my Celestron Omni 32 mm Plössl eyepiece, the true field appears to be about 50 deg at 12 mm.

Second Set of Postulates:

- At 24 mm, the second group is close to the focal plane of the first group. The first group acts as a 2x Barlow lens. The third group acts as a 50 mm eyepiece. The result is effectively a 24 mm eyepiece.
- At 8 mm, the first group moves forwards and gives a little more magnification, perhaps acting as a 3x Barlow. The second group now moves backwards and adds its power to the third group, so the second and third group act like a 25 mm eyepiece. The result is effectively an 8 mm eyepiece.

Configuration at 24 mm:

- The separation of the first and second groups is 40 mm
- The separation of the second and third groups is 40 mm
- The focal length of the first group is –40 mm.
- The telescope focal plane is 17.5 mm after the first group. The first group sends the focal plane 31 mm after the first group. The magnification is about 1.8.
- The second group is 40 mm after the first group, close to the focal plane.
- The combination of the second and third groups give a focal length of 40 mm.

Configuration at 8 mm

- The first group moves forward by 7 mm and the second back by 33 mm.
- The telescope focal plane is now 25 mm after the first group. It sends the focal plane 66 mm after the first group. This is about 20 mm in front of the third group. The magnification is about 2.7.
- The second and third groups have an effective focal length of about 25 mm.
