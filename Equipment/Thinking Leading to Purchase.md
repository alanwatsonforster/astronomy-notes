# Thinking Leading to Purchase of SV70

Written 2022-01 and 2022-02

## Current Equipment

I have an Explore Scientific Sun Catcher 70 mm, a generic version of the Celestron Travelscope 70. The optics aren’t great and the plastic focuser is awful.

## Evolution of Thinking on Telescopes

I live on the edge of the fifth-largest city in the world. So, the best sights available to me locally are the planets, moon, double stars, and brighter deep-sky objects. I want to be able to observe them all.

My SC70 is limited. It can’t really take magnification above 50×, and for planets and the moon one really wants to be at 100× or more. Even at 50×, the optics show significant aberrations. The plastic focuser is unpleasant to use. However, it is very light at about 0.8 kg (with a diagonal but without an eyepiece). I find that with a 24 mm 50 deg eyepiece (3.1 deg at 16×), I don’t need a finder.

I thought about mounting a C90 in parallel. The C90 gives magnifications from 50× to 150× with 25 to 8 mm eyepieces, which are great for viewing the planets. It’s significantly heavier at 2.1 kg. The SC70 would serve as the finder and give magnifications from 16× to 50×. The SC70 (with an actual aperture of about 60 mm aperture) at 16× to 50× gives exit pupils of 3.8 to 1.2 mm. The C90 would give 1.8 mm for the C90 at 50×, so somewhat brighter. The total weight would be about 3 kg.

However, the SC70 is still a bit crap. I thought about replacing it with a Stellarvue 50 mm or 60 mm finder. They are light at about 0.5 kg and very well-built, but according to Matt Wedel they’re really limited to about 30×. Still, that would give 10× to 30× for 24 to 8 mm eyepieces and exit pupils of about 6 at 10× and 2 mm at 30×. The total weight would still be about 3 kg, but I’d have the option to travel with just the finder and a light tripod.

Thinking a little more, another idea occurred. Why not get the SVBONY 80 mm f/7 ED-doublet refractor? The total weight is about 3 kg again and the cost is similar to a C90 with a SV60.

Being f/7 and having an ED doublet, it should be able to manage higher magnifications than faster non-ED telescopes. It might well be able to give magnifications from 20× with a 25 mm eyepiece to 150× with a 4 mm eyepiece, covering much of the range of the C90 plus the longer range of the Stellarvue finder. The field with a 25 mm 60 deg eyepiece would be about 3 deg, so again I’d probably not need a finder. In the range of 20× to 30×, the exit pupil would be 4.0 to 2.7 mm, which would be larger than the 3.0 to 2.0 mm of the Stellarvue 60 mm finder and my current SC70, so give a brighter image. Compared to the Stellarvue finders, I’d lose the range from 10× to 20× and the extreme portability, but this can be partially compensated with my 10×42 monocular.

One doubt in all of this is whether my simple mount can manage 3+ kg of equipment. Certainly, I don’t think any of my ball heads are adequate, since they have problems even with the roughly 1 kg load of the SC70 and zoom eyepiece. I’m fairly sure that the heavy-duty pan-tilt heads would work in altitude-azimuth configuration and possibly a pair could work in equatorial-configuration. One way to determine this would be to attempt to use my PS127, which weighs 3.4 kg.

Another option would be SVBONY 70 mm f/6. This probably has more CA at higher magnifications (since it is f/6 rather than f/7) and has less of an advantage in image brightness, but it only weighs 2.2 kg rather than 3.0 kg. It is more compact, too, and could also serve as a travel telescope.

## Evolution of Thinking on a Telescope for SPM

The main advantages of SPM are seeing and dark skies. This suggests a wide-field refractor capable of high magnifications.

At one point I was thinking of using a Stellarvue 50/60/80 mm finder, but being f/4 and non-ED doublet refractors, they probably can’t achieve high magnification.

A better option might be the SVBONY 70 mm f/6 ED doublet. This is only 2.2 kg and packs down to 320 mm long. It can theoretically achieve about 140×. I will probably see more with this telescope in dark time at SPM than I will with any other telescope in Mexico City.

The SVBONY 80 mm f/7 ED doublet refractor is undoubtedly more capable, but is significantly longer at 465 mm when collapsed and slightly more massive at 3.0 kg. It’s at the upper end of something I’d want to travel with.

I could leave a tripod at SPM and only take the telescope, heads, and accessories. The heads would probably have to be checked.

## Evolution of Thinking on a Telescope for Weekends Away

While I might just about get away with something like the SVBONY 80 mm f/7 ED achromat for SPM, I won’t be able to take anything this large with me on weekends away with my family, when space in the car is at a premium.

However, the SVBONY 70 mm f/6 is no larger than my existing 70 mm f/6, which does travel with me. It is heavier, but that doesn’t matter too much.

Lugenbuhl and Skiff (p. 8) write: “Frequently the sole requirement for the visibility of a notoriously difficult nebula is not a large telescope or a special eyepiece, but a truly dark sky.”

With this in mind, getting a decent travel telescope that I can actually take with me is more important than getting a larger aperture for home, as long as it gives decent views of the planets. So get a 70 mm f/6 first and then maybe something bigger, perhaps a 100 mm ED, for home.

On 2022-02-12, I bought an SVBONY SV503 70ED f/6.
