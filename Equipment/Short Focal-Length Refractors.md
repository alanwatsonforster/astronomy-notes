# Short Focal-Length Refractors

[These are my notes on short focal-length refractors. I eventually bought the SVBONY 70 mm f/6.]

## Orion CT80

- 80 mm f/5
- OTA only
- 1.0 kg
- 80 mm doublet fully coated
- 400 mm focal length
- Aluminum tube.
- Aluminum and plastic 1.25 in focuser
- Vixen or ¼-20 female thread
- USD 109 from Orion
- MXP 4500 from Orion, including shipping and import duties.

Compatible with green-laser finder USD 70\.

## Meade Adventure Scope 80

- 80 mm f/5
- [Reported](https://www.cloudynights.com/topic/585955-meade-adventure-scope-80/) to be the same as the discontinued Orion ST80, which was recommended in “Astrophotography On The Go”
- 1.9 kg
- 80 mm
- 400 mm focal length
- All-metal tube and 1.25 in focuser
- USD 109

How to attach to photographic tripods:

- ST80: Attach an Arca plate to the tube? Problem is that the tube is round and arca plates are typically flat.
- ST80: Attach a Vixen dovetail to the tube like in the ST80-T? Then attach an Arca plate to the dovetail.
  - <https://www.amazon.com/SVBONY-Dovetail-Mounting-Telescope-Equatorial/dp/B07LGMW1YP>
- ST80-T/A: attach Arca plate to Vixen dovetail using ¼-20 holes. For the ST80-T, this is probably the best solution, as it involves no more weight than the other ones.
- ST80-A: replace Vixen dovetail with QR plate
- ST80-T: attach Arca plate plate to tube

How to attach to EQ mount:

- Use ¼-20 adapter
- Attach Vixen saddle to mount using wood and screws.

## Celestron Travelscope 70

- 70 mm f/5.7
- 70 mm diameter
- 400 mm focal length
- Objective is Fraunhofer doublet spaced by foil pads fixed to the flint lens.
- Focuser seems to be slightly better than the one on my Explore Science 70/400.
- Sort of a light version of the ST80 for use on a photographic tripod in parallel with a 60 mm Mak.

## StellarVue 60 mm Finder

- 60 mm f/3.75 (225 mm)
- [https://www.stellarvue.com/1-stellarvue-10x60-finder-scope/](https://www.stellarvue.com/1-stellarvue-10x60-finder-scope/)
- $190 with extra $50 for rings to mount to Vixen dovetail.
- 19.5 oz or 23 oz (0.65 kg) with included 23 mm 50 deg eyepiece.
- With include 23 mm 50 deg eyepiece gives 5 deg at 10x
- Correct image
- C90 with 32 mm gives 1.3 deg at 40x, so quite a jump from 5 deg at 10x. Probably would want a 12 mm to give 20x.
- SV60 with 8-24 zoom would give 4.3-2.1 degrees at 9x-28x. This might be an excellent choice.
- C90 with 8-24 zoom would give 0.8-0.4 deg at 50-150x.
- Review: [https://www.reddit.com/r/telescopes/comments/75n62m/fun\_with\_60mm/](https://www.reddit.com/r/telescopes/comments/75n62m/fun_with_60mm/). Seems to work nicely to at least 32x.

## SVBONY 80 mm f/7 ED-doublet refractor

- 80 mm f/7
- $350 or MX$12000
- 3.0 kg
- <https://www.svbony.com/sv503-80ed-f7-doublet-telescope/>
- [https://www.svbony.com/blog/svbony-sv503-80ed-review/](https://www.svbony.com/blog/svbony-sv503-80ed-review/)
  - “During visual observations of Jupiter with Meade HD-60 6.5 mm (86x) and Levenhuk LER 3 mm (187x) eyepieces, the telescope showed very small chromatic aberration (very weak blue violet halo). 4-5 belts are visible, as well as some details in the belts. At the same time, the sharpness and contrast of the image even at 187x magnification were good – for the Moon, Saturn or Mars, you can confidently use a magnification of up to 200x. A chromatic halo was visible on the star Rigel. Diffraction pattern without any rays and deformations.”
  - “For visual observations, it will definitely please, despite the small aperture.”
  - “The measured weight of the tube with metal rings and caps is 3 kg”
- [https://www.amateurastrophotography.com/sv503-80ed-review-svbony](https://www.amateurastrophotography.com/sv503-80ed-review-svbony)
  - “The tube assembly with tube rings and dovetail bar weighs  2730g.”
  - “With the moon, the images were clear and crisp. With my SVBONY 6mm, 66º eyepiece which combines with the SV503 to produce a magnification of just over 90, the moon nicely filled the field of view. I even tried my 3mm Explore Scientific eyepiece which produces a magnification of 187 and despite this exceeding the age-old 50x per inch of aperture guide for usable magnification, the image was perfectly satisfactory.”
  - “The SV503 produced stunning views of the Red planet, with clear surface features and the glittering polar ice cap being visible. For these observations, my Meade 9.7mm eyepiece with a Celestron 2.5x Luminos-series Barlow lens yielding a magnification of approximately 145 produced the best view. Again, I tried the Explore Scientific 3mm eyepiece, with a magnification of 187, but I found the image less sharp than the lower magnification combination, so maybe that 50x per inch rule of thumb still stands true.”
