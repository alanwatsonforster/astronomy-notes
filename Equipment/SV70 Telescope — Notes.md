# SV70 Telescope — Notes

## Purchase

- Purchased 2022-02-12 from Amazon MX for MXP 10,115.00 (approximately USD 500).

## Notes

The rings came with spacers to hold the telescope further from the dovetail plate. I removed these to reduce the torque on the mount.

## Eyepieces

Initially, I plan to use the telescope with a Celestron Omni 32 mm eyepiece (50 deg apparent FoV), a Celestron 8–24 mm zoom eyepiece (40–60 deg apparent FoV), and a Celestron Omni 2× Barlow lens.

These eyepieces will give the following magnifications, apparent FoV (in degrees), true FoV (in degrees), and EPDs (in mm).

|Eyepiece|Mag|AFoV|TFoV|EPD|
|--|--|--|--|--|
|32 mm|13×|50|3.8|5.3|
|25 mm|17×|50|3.0|4.2|
|15 mm|28×|50|1.8|2.5|
|25 mm with 2×|34×|50|1.5|2.1|
|15 mm with 2×|56×|50|0.9|1.3|
|Zoom at 24 mm|18×|40|2.29|4.0|
|Zoom at 16 mm|26×|50|1.90|2.7|
|Zoom at 12 mm|35×|55|1.57|2.0|

|Zoom at 8 mm with 2×|105×|60|0.57|0.7|

Notes:

- At the end of 2023, I noticed that the zoom is giving double images at high magnifications. I’ll need to check for damage.

- However, the 25 mm and 15 mm, with the 2× Barlow, cover the range of the zoom with a wider field. Of course, they do not cover the range of the zoom with the Barlow lens.

- There isn’t much point in using the 32 mm with the 2× Barlow. The 15 mm or the 8–24 mm zoom at 16 mm gives the same magnification and field.

- Again, there isn’t much point to using the 8–24 mm zoom at 16–24 mm with the 2× Barlow. Using it at 8–12 mm will give wider fields and have less glass in the beam.

- Even with the 2× Barlow, I can’t reach the good-seeing magnification limit of 140×. For that, I’d need the 8–24 mm zoom at 9 mm with a 3× Barlow, a 6 mm with the 2× Barlow, or a 3 mm.

At some point, I might replace these with a set of X-Cel LX eyepieces, which have larger apparent FoV and, at shorter focal lengths, have longer eye-reliefs than the Omnis.

I tended to switch between the 32 mm Omni for finding and the 25 mm E-Lux with the 2x Omni for viewing.

In particular, using the 8-24 mm to view Jupiter at higher magnifications, I saw a lot of purple flare and preferred to use the 25 mm E-Lux with 2x Omni. However, this was lacking in magnification, especially for Saturn.

A cheap upgrade might be a 15 mm Omni ($600), which would give 7.5 mm with the 2x Omni, and overall give me 32, 25, 15, 12.5, and 7.5 mm. The eye-relief of 13 mm is probably fine for me, but perhaps is a little tight for public viewing. Jupiter’s moons fill about ⅓ of the field of the 25 mm and 2x, so would fill about ½ of the field with the 15 mm and 2x.

The 12 mm Omni has insufficient eye-relief. Unfortunately, there is nothing in the Omni line between 15 mm and 32 mm.

More expensive option would be X-Cel LXs ($2000) in similar focal lengths.

I checked the 8-24 mm the next day and confirmed that it has a strong ghost image only slightly displaced from the main image.

## Finder

I originally thought there would be no need for a finder or even a laser pointer. The 32 mm Omni eyepiece has an apparent field of view of 50 degrees, which translates to a true field of view of 3.8 degrees. However, it has become clear that I cannot reliably place even a bright object like Saturn or Jupiter in this field. See the “Iron Sights” document for more discussion of this problem and possible solutions.

## 2023-11-25 Noche de Estrellas

I used this telescope on 2023-11-25 for the Noche de Estrellas.

### Diagonal

Rotating the diagonal with respect to the telescope (e.g., to adjust the height of the eyepiece for children) causes the image to move out of the field of a 12 mm eyepiece. I suspect the diagonal is not aligned.

I checked the diagonal the next day. The prism is held in place by a clip, but has no fine adjustment. I moved the clip and improved the alignment, but it was still noticeably misaligned.

## 2023-11-26 Two-Ring Sight

Moving between Jupiter and Saturn, I discovered that I cannot point by sighting along the tube with sufficient accuracy to get a target in the 3.8 degree field of the 32 mm eyepiece.

I experimented with a two-ring sight: a piece of wood onto which I attached two rolls of black tape, using their inner 30 mm holes as apertures and a 2-inch shaving mirror to allow 90 degree viewing. I spaced the apertures to give a 6 degree field of view. The eye can accurately align two circles, which mitigates parallax. I was able to center Jupiter and Saturn quite easily.

Amazon has $200 [toy periscopes](https://www.amazon.com.mx/UKCOCO-Playground-Periscope-Experimento-Herramientas/dp/B0B3SV8M2Q/ref=sr_1_26?keywords=periscope&qid=1701054271&sr=8-26). These seem to have roughly 25 mm apertures. If one mirror was removed, these might work as more practical implementations of this idea.

On the other hand, straight-through red dot finders are [available](https://www.amazon.com.mx/Akozon-Telescopio-Accesorios-Microscopios-Estrellas/dp/B0C752D2WY/ref=sr_1_8?__mk_es_MX=ÅMÅŽÕÑ&crid=2HSN2FNJF1KD1&keywords=celestron+red+dot&qid=1701091625&sprefix=celestron+red+dot%2Caps%2C151&sr=8-8&ufe=app_do%3Aamzn1.fos.4e545b5e-1d45-498b-8193-a253464ffa47) for about $300.

## 2024-01-12 Two-Ring Sight

I made a two-ring sight from two M6 eye bolts and an inspection mirror attached to allow right-angle viewing. It worked very well. I’d center Jupiter in the sighting rings, and it would appear in the field of the 32 mm eyepiece. See the additional documents.
