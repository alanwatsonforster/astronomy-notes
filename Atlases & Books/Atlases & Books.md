### The Observer’s Sky Atlas (3rd edition)

- Karkoschka  
  - Whole sky to 6.0 mag, but 250 objects to 9.0 mag.  
  - Whole sky at 2.5 mm/deg, but 250 objects at 10 mm/deg.  
  - 14.0 x 21.6 cm (5.5 x 8.5 in)  
  - Black and white.

I downloaded this in PDF from Springer in January 2020, using my UNAM access. (Page 59 appears to be an erroneous duplicate of page 61.)

Printing at half-letter size:

- Select two maps. Print two-up at 125%.  
- Replace sheet in the input tray.  
- Select the two corresponding descriptions. Print two-up at 125%, but in “Layout” select reverse order.  
- Cut the sheet in two.

Things I like at first impression:

- The deeper charts are great for confirming what you see through the eyepiece.  
- The shallow carts are not great for general location; For this, I think you need a planisphere or a chart that shows larger regions of the sky (e.g., PSA).  
- The list of objects seems to be good. The details seem to be useful for planning, but perhaps not at the telescope. Some parts seem confusing.  
- The charts indicate which ones are adjoining, which helps navigation.  
- The lack of color and simple design make it seem ugly compared to the Tirion/Sinnott atlases.

### Cambridge Sky Atlas

- Tirion  
- 6.5 mag  
- 22.9 x 30.5 cm (9.0 x 12.0 in)  
- The size and scale of the maps is excellent for exploring the sky with the naked eye.  
- It’s significantly larger than A4 or US letter, which is less than convenient for carrying it around.  
- I have the spiral-bound version, but unfortunately it doesn’t fold flat back on itself. This makes it somewhat inconvenient to use, as you need to use both hands.  
- As with many atlases, it seems to have a lot of text on basic astronomical concepts. I’d be happy with a brief introduction, the 4 pages of maps of the Moon, the 20 pages of maps of the sky, 1 page for key to the charts, and indices to the constellations, Messier objects, and Caldwell objects. So, perhaps 30 to 35 pages in total. Instead, the book has 90 pages, so to me more than half of it is irrelevant.  
- Printing the Moon with S on the top seems odd if most of your clients are in the Northern Hemisphere.
